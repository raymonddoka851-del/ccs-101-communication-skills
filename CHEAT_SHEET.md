# CCS 101 · Exam-day review

For most answers use: **define → explain the process or principle → apply it to an example → evaluate or conclude**.

## Communication and collaboration

- Process: **source/participant → encode → message → channel → receiver → decode → feedback**, shaped by context and possible noise.
- Do not confuse message (content) with channel (medium).
- 7 Cs: clarity, completeness, conciseness, consistency, correctness, consideration, courtesy.
- Listening: attend, interpret, check meaning (paraphrase / clarify), respond.
- Group work: shared goal, roles, deadlines, communication, integration, review.
- Oral presentation: audience and purpose → organized points/evidence → rehearse → deliver clearly → respond to questions.
- Reading: **skimming** for overview; **scanning** for a detail; close reading to evaluate an argument.

## Study and information literacy

- SQ3R: Survey, Question, Read, Recite/Recall, Review.
- Active revision: retrieve from memory, check, correct, schedule delayed practice.
- Evaluate sources: author, evidence, relevance, currency, purpose/perspective; record citation details as you take notes.
- Paraphrase genuinely and cite borrowed ideas; changing a few words is not sufficient.

## Writing

- Process: plan → draft → revise meaning/structure → edit sentences → proofread details.
- Paragraph: topic sentence + support/evidence/example + explanation/link.
- Essay: focused introduction/thesis → ordered body points → conclusion that answers the task.
- Quote sparingly and accurately; acknowledge sources in the required style.

## Common mark-losing errors

- Listing terms without explaining or applying them.
- Assuming communication is one-way or that the audience interprets every message identically.
- Treating rereading as proof of recall.
- Making claims without evidence or citation.
- Editing spelling while leaving an unclear argument unchanged.
