# 04 · Writing Skills

## Writing is a process

Effective academic writing communicates a focused purpose to a particular reader. It is usually produced through **planning → drafting → revising → editing → proofreading**. Revising improves meaning and organization; editing improves sentence clarity and correctness; proofreading catches final surface errors.

## Build a clear paragraph

A useful paragraph normally has:

1. **Topic sentence:** states the paragraph’s main point.
2. **Support:** explains the point with evidence, reasoning, or an example.
3. **Link:** connects the evidence to the claim or transitions to the next point.

**Example skeleton:** *Claim:* Active recall supports revision. *Evidence/example:* A student answers a question from memory before checking notes. *Explanation:* This reveals what can be retrieved and what still needs review.

## Essay structure

- **Introduction:** establish context, define key terms if needed, and state the focus or thesis.
- **Body:** organize paragraphs around distinct points; support claims with relevant evidence and explain its significance.
- **Conclusion:** answer the task directly, synthesize the main reasoning, and avoid introducing unsupported new claims.

Use transitions to show relationships—contrast, cause, sequence, or addition—not merely to decorate the page.

## Academic integrity and sources

Keep traceable source details while researching. Distinguish quotation, paraphrase, and your own analysis. Quote exact words accurately and sparingly; paraphrase by genuinely restating the idea in your own structure and language; cite borrowed ideas and wording using the required style. A citation does not excuse a misleading or unsupported claim.

## Editing checklist

- [ ] Does each paragraph serve the question and one main point?
- [ ] Are claims supported and sources acknowledged?
- [ ] Are sentences direct, complete, and logically connected?
- [ ] Are grammar, spelling, names, figures, and references checked?
- [ ] Does the final format follow the assignment instructions?
