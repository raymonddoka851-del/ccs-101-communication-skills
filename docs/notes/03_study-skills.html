Short-Term Memory (STM): Holds limited information for a brief period (about 20-30 seconds). Immediate memory is part of STM, storing around 7 chunks of data Long-Term Memory (LTM): Has unlimited capacity and duration. Proper encoding and organization are essential for efficient retrieval of stored information.

Attention is required to transfer information to short-term memory .

Importance of study skills Effective study skills improve academic performance, reduce stress, and foster lifelong learning habits. Academic Success : Study skills improve ability to learn, organization, retention, and application of knowledge, crucial for thriving in self-directed college learning. Life Skills : Study skills extend beyond academics, improving time management, organization, and problem-solving in various life areas .

--- slide 2 ---

Stress Reduction : Effective strategies reduce last- minute cramming and help students cope with academic stress. Help increase your confidence, self-esteem, and reduce anxiety about tests, exams and meeting assignment deadlines. NB: D eveloping solid study skills early on can make a big difference in students’ academic performance and personal growth. Study skills are crucial not only for managing coursework but also for balancing other areas of life.

--- slide 2 ---

Expected Learning Outcomes Apply effective time-management and study techniques to improve academic performance . Engage actively with learning materials by asking questions, reflecting, and connecting ideas. Set academic priorities, develop study plans, and use planning tools to avoid last-minute cramming and procrastination. Identify personal learning preferences and adapt study strategies to individual strengths and needs .

--- slide 2 ---

Definition Studying is the process of acquiring knowledge or skills through reading, reflection, and practice to enhance understanding, retention, recall and application. Study skills are techniques, methods, habits and approaches that enhance learning. They help students receive, process, organize, comprehend, retain , recall and apply information efficiently in different circumstances .

--- slide 2 ---

Memory Techniques Use acronyms - a word that is made up by taking the first letters of all the key words or ideas you need to remember and creating a new word out of them. Acronym (ROYGBIV) -the colour spectrum Make up a Sentence in which the first letter of each word is part of or represents the initial of what you want to remember. For example mnemonics of Richard Of York Gave Battle In Vain to remember the order of the colours of a rainbow Visual image – Associate a visual image with a word or name to help you remember them better. Chunking grouping different bits of information together into more manageable or meaningful chunks .

Memory process Encoding: Interpreting information in a meaningful way. Without encoding, information cannot be stored effectively. Storage : Organizing and repeating information so it can be placed into long-term memory (LTM). Active engagement is necessary for effective storage . Retrieval : Accessing information from LTM. Effective retrieval depends on how well the information was stored.

Overall here are 10 Ways to Make an Effective Study Plan/schedule Know your learning style – Every student has a different learning style hence know when, where, and how you study best, and tailor your plan around that. IMPROVING CONCENTRATION Concentration is divided concentration into three levels: light, moderate, and deep. There are three types of concentration problems: difficulty focusing at will, difficulty sustaining focus over a period of time, and difficulty limiting focus to one task at a time . The real causes of most concentration problems are lack of attention, lack of interest, and lack of motivation

ACTIVE LEARNING Distinguish between active and passive learning. passive learning, The student listens to lectures, takes notes, memorizes and reproduces the material for the required assessments without really reflecting upon it . active learning The student becomes much more involved in the material being presented, critically evaluating it, thinking through the implications, noting questions and issues, and trying to relate it to other observations or information of their own .
